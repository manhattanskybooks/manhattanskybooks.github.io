The book

Sunday, 7:00 a.m.

A pill on a stranger's nightstand pulls a fixer for New York's wealthiest families into the hundred-and-ten-story tower where he works, and into the business his employers have been running on Sundays since 1903.

In November 1903, on a pier where the Cathedral now stands, Cormac Kane wrote forty-one names in a ledger and put the same word beside every one of them.

Spoiled.

Sunday, 7:00 a.m. A hundred and twenty-three years later, the Cathedral seals itself, every shutter, every fire curtain, every damper, with two people inside who were never meant to be there.

Dominique Silva is an engineer who asked one question about four floors drawing power they shouldn't.

Marcus Reed is the fixer the family sends when a question like that needs to go away. Eleven years of making rich people's disasters vanish. This morning he learned his own brother was one of them.

They have sixty-four minutes before a solvent fire takes out four floors and every piece of evidence on them. A hundred stories of locked stairwell between them and the room where it gets triggered. Four teams in the well, coming down.

Nobody is coming for them. The Commissioner of Police locked the doors.

The author

J. Carter Hunt

J. Carter Hunt grew up outside Detroit, where he made his living as a musician. He moved west and built a second career in business. He writes crime fiction now, from the Nevada desert.

The Cathedral is his first novel, and the cornerstone of more to come.

Where it came from

For decades his work took him to the big cities, and inside their towers. He spent a lot of time in those buildings, usually with access to one floor or two, and never the rest. In every city he noticed the same thing: a tower with a single name across the top, thousands of people walking past it and through it every morning without a second look, and nobody asking what was on the floors they couldn't reach. Then a second question found him. Some of the most trusted companies in the world began a century ago as a few men, a wagon and a cellar, delivering goods in a way that couldn't possibly have paid. So what else were they carrying? The Cathedral is what happened when the two questions met.
